Design decisions

The landing page leads with a live board, not a headline

The hero is the product — a working leaderboard with a real gap line. Someone who has never heard of MaxPoll understands it in two seconds without reading. The "2,412 voting right now" eyebrow does the social proof job that a wall of feature copy usually fails at.

The timer

Inverted to dark — it's the only dark block on a paper-white page, so it pulls the eye without needing colour noise. A gold progress ring plus a monospaced 04:12:07 countdown, with a red bar draining along the bottom edge. Under one hour the whole block shifts to a deep red gradient and the ⏳ pulses. Countdown digits are monospaced so the block doesn't twitch every second.

Counts everywhere, detail behind ₹9

Every card shows 🗳️ 340 votes and 👥 28 options. Totals are free — they're proof of life and they create the urge. Per-option splits and names are the paid layer.

Activity is the return engine

With no push notifications on web, this screen is what pulls people back — so the badge count sits in the header on every screen. The highest-converting row is "9 people voted exactly like you" with two names visible and the rest blurred: it's personal, it's curiosity about specific people, and one name showing proves it's real rather than a tease.

Premium comes from restraint

  • One motion. Rank rows sliding on recompute. Everything else is still. Scattered animation reads as amateur; one orchestrated moment reads as considered.
  • Three colours with jobs. Gold = rank 1. Violet = movement. Red = time. Nothing decorative.
  • Archivo 900 for titles at tight tracking, monospace for every number.
  • Real numbers everywhere — 1,240 members, 18 live polls, 2,412 voting. Density signals scale.
  • iOS emoji only — set font-family:'Apple Color Emoji' first, and ship an SBIX fallback so Android renders the same glyphs. Mixed emoji sets are the fastest way to look like a side project.

The disclosure line is load-bearing

🔓 "Votes on MaxPoll are public. Your name will be visible on this poll." — on the landing page, in the join sheet before the first vote, and on the poll itself. This is what separates selling depth from selling a betrayal, and it's the difference between a business and an FTC case. Do not make it small.
